Kedarkantha Trek Height: How High Is Kedarkantha Peak?

The Kedarkantha Trek height is approximately 3,810 metres (12,500 feet) above sea level. Located in the Govind Wildlife Sanctuary area of Uttarkashi district, Uttarakhand, Kedarkantha is a popular Himalayan trek known for its snow-covered trails, pine forests, mountain views, and scenic summit.

Understanding the altitude is important before starting the trek because the route involves a significant elevation gain from the base village to the summit. With proper preparation, gradual ascent, and suitable trekking equipment, Kedarkantha can be an accessible Himalayan adventure for many beginners and experienced trekkers.

What Is the Height of Kedarkantha Peak?

The height of Kedarkantha Peak is around 3,810 metres or 12,500 feet above sea level.

The summit provides panoramic views of several Himalayan peaks and surrounding valleys. During the winter season, snow can cover much of the upper trail, creating the classic Kedarkantha winter trekking experience.

Kedarkantha Trek Altitude Gain

One of the interesting aspects of the Kedarkantha Trek is the gradual increase in altitude as you move from the lower Himalayan region toward the summit.

A typical route starts from Sankri, a popular base village for several treks in Uttarakhand. From Sankri, the trail gradually climbs through forests, meadows, and mountain camps before reaching Kedarkantha Summit.

The approximate altitude progression may look like this:

Sankri: Around 1,900–2,000 m
Juda Ka Talab: Around 2,700 m
Kedarkantha Base: Around 3,400–3,500 m
Kedarkantha Summit: Around 3,810 m

Exact elevations can vary slightly depending on the route and source.

Why Is Kedarkantha Height Important for Trekkers?

Altitude is an important consideration on any Himalayan trek. As elevation increases, the air becomes thinner and the amount of available oxygen decreases.

On the Kedarkantha Trek, the altitude gain is gradual, but trekkers can still experience symptoms such as:

Headache
Tiredness
Shortness of breath
Reduced appetite
Difficulty sleeping
Mild dizziness

These symptoms can have different causes, and persistent or worsening symptoms should not be ignored.

The best approach is to walk at a comfortable pace, stay hydrated, eat properly, avoid overexertion, and allow your body time to adjust to the increasing altitude.

Kedarkantha Trek Height From Sankri

Sankri is generally considered the starting point for the Kedarkantha Trek. At roughly 1,900–2,000 metres, it is significantly lower than the Kedarkantha summit.

From Sankri, trekkers gradually gain around 1,800–1,900 metres of elevation before reaching the summit at approximately 3,810 metres.

This elevation gain is one reason the trek requires reasonable physical fitness even though Kedarkantha is often chosen by beginners.

Route-Wise Kedarkantha Trek Altitude

The exact itinerary can differ between trekking operators, but a commonly followed route includes the following major points:

1. Sankri – Base Village

Sankri is the starting point for the trek and is located at approximately 1,900–2,000 metres. Trekkers generally reach Sankri by road before beginning the walking portion of the journey.

2. Juda Ka Talab

The trail from Sankri climbs through beautiful Himalayan forests toward Juda Ka Talab. The campsite is situated at roughly 2,700 metres.

In winter, the surrounding landscape can become covered in snow.

3. Kedarkantha Base Camp

The route continues toward the Kedarkantha Base Camp, which is located at approximately 3,400–3,500 metres.

This section offers beautiful views of the surrounding Himalayan landscape and prepares trekkers for the summit climb.

4. Kedarkantha Summit

The final climb leads to Kedarkantha Peak at approximately 3,810 metres (12,500 feet).

The summit section can be physically demanding, particularly during winter when snow and icy trail conditions may increase the difficulty.

What Is the Best Time to Trek to Kedarkantha?

Kedarkantha is particularly famous as a winter trek. The months from December to March are popular for snow trekking, although actual snowfall and trail conditions can vary each year.

During winter, temperatures can drop significantly, especially at higher camps and during the early-morning summit climb.

If you want to experience snowy landscapes, check the latest weather and trail conditions before choosing your trekking dates.
